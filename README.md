It is the discovery of

1. ancient Egyptian arithmetic;
2. the medieval Quadrivium, and its teaching of Arithmetic and Euclidean/synthetic geometry as interconnected but separate disciplines;
3. decomposition method and equal additions method as the venerable subtraction procedures of history used by adult mathematicians, where decomposition is more intuitive with either of manipulatives and non-positional numeral systems, but equal additions is more efficient with combination of positional numeral system and use of paper and ink rather than manipulatives.
4. lattice multiplication, the venerable multiplication procedure for paper and ink medium, before long multiplication was invented and promoted over it because the latter takes less space on paper compared to the former.
5. galley division, the venerable division procedure for paper and ink medium, before long division was invented and promoted over it because the latter eliminates the messiness of the former.
6. Russian peasant multiplication, one of two ancient Egyptian multiplication procedures preserved by Russian peasants in the 19th century.
7. Austrian method of subtraction, which is the longest-lasting of the subtraction procedures invented specifically to suit primary school math education. It is a 19th century variant of the Equal additions method still in use in Germany and Austria. The variant maintained the notation, but reversed the scripted question of "first operand minus second operand equals what?" to asking "second operand plus what equals the first?"
8. former Russian math curriculum based on A. P. Kiselev's books in the early 20th century for junior and senior secondary school, and its long-term teaching of Euclidean/synthetic geometry
9. Russian variant of standard decomposition procedure - invented in Kiselev's Arithmetic, the variant uses dots above digits of the first operand, instead of cancelling and writing 10s and 9s on top of first operand. it only requires student to remember that a dot on top of a non-zero digit means to decrease digit by 1, but a dot on top of a zero digit means to interpret digit as a 9. Has advantage that the exact same annotations can be reinterpreted with Austrian method, in which a dot on top of any column means to increase bottom digit by 1.
10. the backlash to the attempted rewriting of the foundations of modern math in 1930s and 1960s, from counting numbers and Euclidean/synthetic geometry to infinite set theory.

Here modern math refers to math

- after the acceptance of the Fourier series and its initial contradictions;
- after the 19th-century acceptance of non-Euclidean geometries as equally valid geometries as Euclidean geometry.
- after the creation of the category of transcendental numbers, which is such that its members are not required to be computable, or attached to synthetic geometry, or attached to previously known math functions.
- which treats number bases as arbitrary rather than dependent on physical realities, commonly human anatomy of 10 fingers or 12 knuckles on non-thumb fingers of a single hand.

# Early Algebra Highlighting Proposal

## What is Early Algebra?

Early algebra refers to arithmetic and the subset of algebra topics taught in primary and secondary schools. Strictly speaking, it excludes

- arithmetic of small whole numbers by manual counting.
- arithmetic of whole numbers represented by non-positional numeral systems. Examples of such systems are the following two systems I first heard of from Prof N. J. Wildberger:
  - use of I as symbol for 1, and use of mutiple Is for every other number.
  - use of a simplified Roman numeral system, in which I stands for one, X for ten, C for hundred, and M for thousand. In canonical form, the symbols are listed in
    decreasing order of value, and each symbol occurs less than 10 times.
    - E.g. I for 1, IIII for 4, IIIIIIIII for 9, X for 10, XI for 11, XXXXIIIII for 45, XXXXX for 50, CII for 102, MXXXXIII for 1043
    - NB: use of this system may require circling times symbol of multiplication in order to differentiate multiplication operator from numeral for ten.
    - NB: use of this system may require teaching full roman numeral system later on for completeness sake.
- the topics of simplification of algebraic expressions, generation of algebraic expressions and other susbsequent topics in algebra.

For teens and adults, this proposal permits the introduction of less optimal but more intuitive arithmetic procedures to the students. It also permits the presentation of the early algebra topics in a different arrangement from that for pre-teens, in order to better suit the challenges and opportunities presented by teens and adults.

## Goals

The goal of early algebra for students is proficiency in evaluation of algebraic expressions involving rational numbers.

For students who are teens or adults, there is an additional goal which is to be able to practice and become proficient in early algebra by themselves, with almost no supervision.

## Guidelines

- The proposal is based on the belief in the sufficiency of existing primary school and secondary school topics for teaching early algebra.
- The proposal recognizes that it is school authorities who have the final decision to allow alternatives to the standard procedures, which differ from what teachers expect of students in a given locality, to show up on answer sheets.

## Arithmetic Topics

The following activities are assumed to constitute arithmetic for rational numbers as taught in school or online:

- Decimal nature of almost numeral systems invented by humans, ie number base is equal to 10, or a multiple of 10 (i.e. 20 and 60).
- Positional nature of standard numeral system, ie Hindu-Arabic numeral system.
- Addition
- Subtraction
- Multiplication with counting number multiplier
- Division
- Fractions
- _of_ operator, for taking fractions of quantities
- Ratios
- Divisibility and Factorization
- Powers and Roots
- Comparison and Number Line

Note that negative rational numbers, decimal numbers and evaluation of arithmetic expressions are treated under algebra, following lead of Singapore math curriculum. Also multiplication which covers fraction multipliers is postponed to algebra, and _of_ operator takes its place in the meantime in arithmetic.

The arithmetic parts of early algebra proposal for adults seek to leverage reference materials (capable of being memorized in school settings), to quickly increase confidence in the study of arithmetic. For this reason the arithmetic parts is recorded in the form of tables, worked examples, formulas, pictorial demonstrations of arithmetic procedures, multi-choice questions, and exercises with detailed solutions. ### Rational Number Categorizations

Early algebra proposal for adults depends on using formulas to perform arithmetic operations whenever possible. And these formulas depend on the perspective taken of the operands involved. Therefore the proposal requires making students aware that

- numbers come in kinds or types, e.g. whole numbers, non-whole numbers, integers, non-integers, positive numbers, negative numbers.
- numbers have parts, e.g. sign, magnitude, numerator, denominator.
- numbers can be presented in different formats, e.g. common fraction, mixed fraction, decimal expansion, percentages, standard form.

### Alternative Arithmetic Procedures

NB: can serve as precursors and/or crosschecking avenues to the ones approved in school, given that the school procedures are often faster.

- Alternative/Precursor to standard decomposition procedure of whole numbers
  - Equal Additions methods, i.e. either original method or Austrian method or a custom variant described below, in increasing order of preference (hence if one decides to adopt an equal additions method, this Early Algebra proposal recommends starting with the custom variant described below). This Early Algebra proposal considers the greater opposition this method offers to treating subtraction as a "take away from" operation which is as fundamental as addition rather than treating addition as more fundamental than subtraction, the greater opposition this method offers to treating base 10 as an arbitrary number base rather than as a necessary feature of arithmetic, and the greater opposition this method offers to treating place value concept as fundamental rather than as abstraction of the workings of base 10, compared to decomposition worthwhile tradeoffs for higher time and space efficiency, elimination of cascading trades, and greater protection against inversion errors. _Can be complementary method introduced before or after standard decomposition procedure done with positional numeral system, and done on paper and ink rather than with manipulatives_.
    - Can adopt a custom variant of original Equal Additions method which this Early Algebra proposal calls the "Cancel Galore" method of subtraction as precursor to whichever subtraction procedure a school teaches for permanent use. This Cancel Galore method aims to trade off efficiency and neatness a little bit, for better protection against inversion errors and switching errors than offered by either the original Equal additions method or the Austrian method. It works as follows: after stacking operands on top of each other and right aligned, ensure that in each operand adjacent digits are separated by enough space from each other. to ensure this, draw a vertical line from top of first operand to bottom of second operand through each space between adjacent digits. Then perform a preparation phase by checking the digits of the first operand from right to left (very important to begin check from right rather than from left, so that an updated cell can be analyzed again). anytime a digit is found which is smaller than the digit below it, draw a diagonal line across the cell of that digit through the cell's contents, and across the cell of the bottom digit left of the digit below it through the cell's contents. above the top digit crossed write the sum of it and 10. below the bottom digit crossed write the sum of it and 1. The goal of the preparation phase is that top cell numbers are larger or equal to bottom digits in each column. Then draw usual horizontal line below second operand and any digits written below it, and perform digit by digit subtraction in any column order (can just stick to right to left order) to obtain the answer.
  - Ancient Egyptian subtraction - basically the equivalent of standard decomposition procedure for non-positional numeral system. It works by cancelling on both sides, and expanding higher token into tens of next smaller token if needed.
- Alternative/Precursor to long multiplication procedure of whole numbers
  - lattice multiplication - leverages multiplication tables and positional numeral system. this Early Algebra proposal considers the lower space efficiency of this method compared to long multiplication as a worthwhile tradeoff for higher time efficiency, greater traceability of carries and lower demands on working memory for primary school children. _Can replace long multiplication procedure._
    - can reduce dependency on multiplication tables, by generating rows to right of lattice grid containing first 5 multiples of each unique digit in the operand represented vertically. then carry out standard addition procedure in memory to compute next 4 multiples on a as-needed basis.
  - Russian peasant multiplication - actually one of two ancient Egyptian multiplication procedures, and hence has advantage of not depending on multiplication tables and not depending on positional numeral system. rather it depends only on halving, doubling and adding. It works as follows: Have table of 2 columns, and fill initially with smaller operand in left column and other operand in right colum. Then double in right column, but halve in the left column until it becomes 1 while dropping remainders. Mark out rows in which remainders were dropped. Finally and add up numbers in right column of rows which have been marked. NB:
    - for division by 2 using positional numeral system, can use short division.
    - at any point that one knows what the product of a row is, one can just skip the doubling and halving and just write out the final row of 1 and the product.
- Alternative/Precursor to long division
  - Division by chunking, aka division by partial quotients method, "Big 7" method - _Can replace long division involving multi-digit divisors._ NB:
    - may employ first 5 multiples of divisor for its table of chunks.
    - ancient Egyptian division and long division can be seen as variants of division by chunking which employ different contents of table of chunks (multiples of divisor by 10 and 2 which are less than or equal to divided, versus first 9 multiples). Long division also differs in being unforgiving if a chunk is selected for reducing the current remainder which is less than the maximum chunk possible.
    - can work even for operands which are positive fractions or positive decimals.
  - Short division - assumes divisor is a single digit. leverages positional numeral system and multiplication tables. _Can be complementary method introduced before or after any division procedure involving multi-digit divisors._
  - Ancient Egyptian division - has advantage of not depending on multiplication tables and not depending on positional number system. It works as follows: Have a table of chunks, initially with 1 in left column, and divisor in right column. Then continue by successive doubling up to maximum product less than or equal to the dividend. Then use repeated subtraction of entries in the right column from dividend, to identify partial quotients in left column to add up to obtain final answer.
    - for division by 1 can teach shortcut to avoid division procedure entirely.
    - for division by 2 can teach shortcut of halving operation used in ancient Egyptian multiplication.
    - if using this procedure with positional numeral system, can add this step before successive doubling step: continue filling with products resulting from first multiplying divisor by 10 up to maximum product less than or equal to the dividend.

## Algebra Topics

- justification for any unproven arithmetic procedures and rules, following lead of Kiselev's Arithmetic.
- the optimized versions of the arithmetic procedures for large counting numbers.
  - standard addition procedure
  - standard american variant of decomposition method, austrian method or original equal additions method; or russian variants of decomposition and equal additions methods.
  - long multiplication or lattice method
  - short division for single digit divisors, and either long division or "Big 7" method for multi-digit divisors
- swapping "of" operator with overloaded meaning of multiplication which covers fraction and negative multipliers. And use bare times symbol and retire use of circled times symbol for multiplication.
- the optimized versions of the arithmetic procedures for fractions.
- Divisibility Rules and Prime Factorization
- developing proficiency in arithmetic of integers and rational numbers involving negative numbers.
- Conversion of positive fractions from and to decimal expansions
- Conversion of positive fractions and decimal expansions to rounded decimal expansions
- Conversion of fractions and decimal expansions from and to percentages
- expression evaluation (aka "substitution") mainly involving only the four basic arithmetic operations
  - includes drilling in convention of concatenation for multiplication
  - can include powers, roots, trigonometry and logarithms
- equation solving involving mainly linear equations
  - introduce guess and check as a last resort strategy
  - can include quadratic equations, and simultaneous equations in two variables
  - can introduce precursor to quadratic expression factorization and quadratic equation solving, which is finding two integers which add up to a given value, and which have another given value as their product.

The climax of the algebra part of early algebra proposal is algebraic expression evaluation and equation solving.
